Methodology

The Gregory Archive includes a large collection of audio and visual materials collected over nearly 60 years from many locations around the world.

This part of the archive focuses on a special group of nearly 30,000 color slides. Each slide is individually mounted and stored in metal file boxes using a system created by Dr. Timothy Gregory. Most boxes are labeled by location—for example, Venice, Monemvasia, or Saqqara—but some are organized by topic, such as Coins, Byzantine Pottery, or Ikons, or by his academic work, like Isthmia 96, APKAS, or Study Tour 1985.  To view the slides as Gregory sorted them, choose Slide Boxes in the menu above. To see the entire collection choose Gallery.

How the Slides are Digitized

To digitize the slides, we used a Nikon D60 camera with a zoom lens (set to 32mm), mounted on a copy stand. A bright LED light panel with a white plastic diffuser sits underneath the slides to light them evenly from behind. This setup also helps show the handwritten notes on the paper or plastic mounts. Each slide is individually positioned and photographed by a team of student archivists.

Once a box of slides is photographed, the images are cropped and automatically adjusted for color and lighting.

Other undergraduate student interns then transcribe the notes written on each slide into a spreadsheet. Spelling mistakes are corrected to make it easier to search the collection. The final data is connected to the images when we upload everything to our online archive.

We also save backups of the images—both the original and the edited versions—on cloud storage at Michigan State University, as well as on other secure storage devices.

Each member of the student internship team (Leli Brown, Morgan Manuszak, Kailey Reed, Kelly Sullivan, and Rebecca Sun) is credited in the metadata of each of the specific images on which they worked.
