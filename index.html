History of Collection

When Dr. Timothy Gregory retired from The Ohio State University, he donated his personal research archive to Michigan State University. This valuable collection includes copies of his publications, lectures, field notes, and other materials gathered during more than 60 years of archaeological research and teaching. Today, this collection is known as the Gregory Archive.

The first part of the archive to be digitized and shared with the public is a set of 35mm color slides. As these images will show, Dr. Gregory was a skilled photographer. From his early trips to Europe to his archaeological work and travels through the cities he lived in, he always carried a camera.

These photographs offer a glimpse into his unique view of the world. They may be helpful for students, researchers, or anyone who enjoys exploring history through images.

Please note:
These images were taken between 1960 and 2012 and reflect Dr. Gregory’s work, travels, and teaching over the years. Timothy Gregory

The Gregory Archive is named after Timothy E Gregory, professor emeritus in the Department of History at Ohio State University. He is well known for his work in the history and archaeology of the Byzantine period, which is sometimes known as the "dark ages."

Gregory led important excavations and survey projects in various parts of Greece and Cyprus, but was especially active at the site of Isthmia, where he served as director for over three decades. To learn more about the excavations, visit the official site of the MSU Excavations at Isthmia.

In addition to his work  as director at Isthmia, Gregory also helped to develop the Chronotype system for analyzing ancient ceramics, started the Museum of Classical Archaeology at Ohio State University and famously organized public performances of Byzantine music and history, known as "Evening at Byzantium." Gregory now lives in the village of Ancient Corinth in Greece.
